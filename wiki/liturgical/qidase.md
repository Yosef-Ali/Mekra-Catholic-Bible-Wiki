## What this page is

This is the **order of service (ሥርዓት) of the Divine Liturgy as actually celebrated at KMCC**, in the Ge'ez Catholic tradition, reconstructed from the parish's own liturgy slides. It is the *rite* — the sequence of texts the priest (ካህን / ይ.ካ.), deacon (ዲያቆን / ይ.ዲ.), and people (ሕዝብ / ይ.ሕ.) actually say and sing.

For the **doctrine** of the Mass — what it *is*, why it is a sacrifice, the real presence — see [[teaching/the-mass]]. For the **practical "what do I do at each part"** see [[teaching/practical-mass]]. This page sits between them: it documents the concrete Ge'ez-rite text, part by part, and points you to the exact slide in the raw source.

The source deck exists in three parallel versions — **Ge'ez, edited edition** (243 slides — `qidase-geez-v2.md`, the current reference used below), **Ge'ez, original** (277 slides — `qidase-geez.md`, kept for history; the edited edition trims and revises roughly 40% of its lines), and **Amharic** (173 slides, the vernacular). All are the same rite; the Ge'ez is longer because it carries more of the chanted propers.

## Which rite, which anaphora

This is a **Ge'ez Rite Catholic** liturgy — the ancient Ethiopian *Qiddase* structure, in full communion with Rome (see the Ge'ez vs. Latin Rite note in [[teaching/the-mass]]). Its signature is the **anaphora** system: the Ethiopian tradition has fourteen anaphoras (Eucharistic prayers), and a celebration selects one.

The edited Ge'ez deck (v2) explicitly titles **both anaphoras it bundles**, resolving what was previously unconfirmed:

1. **The Anaphora of the Apostles (የአባቶቻችን የቅዱሳን ሐዋርያት የቁርባን ጸሎት)** — titled at slide 129, running to slide 182.
2. **The Anaphora of Our Lady (የቅድስት ድንግል ማርያም የቁርባን ጸሎት)** — titled at slide 183, running to the end (slide 243).

Each is a **complete, self-contained Eucharistic Prayer** — its own Sanctus, institution narrative, epiclesis, and Lord's Prayer — because in the Ge'ez Rite only one anaphora is prayed per Mass, chosen by the celebrant. The deck bundles both as alternatives rather than presenting one as "the" ordinary text.

## Key texts (verbatim from the source)

**The institution narrative — the Body** (v2 slide 199, within the Anaphora of Our Lady — the celebrant speaks this key line in Amharic even though the surrounding deck is Ge'ez):

> ወደ ሰማይ፤ ወደ አንተ፤ ወደ አባቱ ራሱን ቀና አደረገ አባቱን አማለደ፡፡ ደቀመዛሙርቱን ከክፉ ሁሉ እንዲጠብቃቸው አደራ አለው፤ ራሱ ቡሩክ ሲሆን ባርኮ፤ ራሱ ቅዱስ ሲሆን ቀድሶ፤ ቆርሶም ለደቀመዛሙርቱ እንዲህ ሲል ሰጣቸው፤ እንካችሁ ሁላችሁም ከሱ ብሉ ለዓለም ሁሉ ቤዛ ስለእናንተ የሚሰጥ ይህ ሥጋዬ ነው፡፡
> — `raw/liturgical/qidase/qidase-geez-v2.md`, slide 199

The people's answer (v2 slide 200):

> አሜን አሜን አሜን እናምናለን እንታመንማለን ጌታችንና አምላካችን ሆይ እናመሰግንሃለን ይህ ሥጋህ መሆኑን በውነት እናምናለን፡፡

**The institution narrative — the Blood** (v2 slide 200, continuing):

> እንዲሁም ከእራት በኋላ ጽዋውን ተመለከተ እንዲህም አለ እንካችሁ ሁሉም ከሱ ጠጡ ስለ እናንተ ለኃጢአት ይቅርታ የሚፈስ ይህ ጽዋ ደሜ ነው፡፡

The people's answer (v2 slide 201):

> አሜን አሜን አሜን እናምናለን እንታመንማለን ጌታችንና አምላካችን ሆይ እናመሰግንሃለን ይህ ደምህ መሆኑን በውነት እናምናለን።

And immediately after, the anamnesis (v2 slide 201):

> ይህን ስታደርጉ የሞትን መታሰቢያ ታደርጋላችሁ፤ የኔን ትንሣኤም ትናገራላችሁ፡፡

These are the words of consecration — the same *"this is my body / this is my blood"* the whole Church has prayed since the Last Supper (cf. [[teaching/the-mass]], on the one sacrifice re-presented). The Anaphora of the Apostles (v2 slides 163–165) carries a parallel but distinct pre-communion prayer at the equivalent point in its own sequence — see **Open questions** on reconciling the two anaphoras' institution wording line by line.

## The Marian character

Ethiopian eucharistic piety is deeply Marian, and this deck shows it: the salutation to Our Lady near the opening (ሰአሊ ለነ ድንግል ማርያም ወላዲተ አምላክ), and above all the full **Anaphora of Our Lady** (Am slide 138 / Ge v1 slide 143 / Ge v2 slide 183), which frames the offering with praise of the Theotokos:

> ልቤ መልካም ነገርን ያፈልቃል፤ እኔም የድንግልን ምስጋናና ትልቅነትዋን እናገራለሁ …
> — Amharic slide 139 (opening of the Anaphora of Our Lady)

On the doctrine behind this, see [[figures/ማርያም]] and the Marian teaching pages; on how it compares to EOTC/Orthodox eucharistic practice, see [[comparative/eucharist]].
