## Overview

የሐዋርያት ሥራ (The Acts of the Apostles) is the second volume of Luke's two-part work, continuing the narrative begun in the Gospel of Luke (ሉቃስ 1:1–4; የሐዋርያት 1:1). Addressed to the same Theophilus, Acts traces the explosive growth of the early Church from the Ascension of Christ through the descent of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost, the missionary journeys of Paul, and the arrival of the Gospel in Rome — the capital of the Gentile world. Traditionally dated c. AD 80–85, Acts is our only canonical history of the apostolic era, bridging the ministry of Jesus with the life of the Pauline churches.

Luke structures Acts around Christ's programmatic promise in Acts 1:8 — "you shall be my witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth." The book unfolds in three geographic movements: the Jerusalem mission (chs. 1–7), the Judean and Samaritan expansion (chs. 8–12), and the Pauline mission to the Gentiles culminating in Rome (chs. 13–28). At the center of this geography stands Pentecost (ch. 2), where the promised Holy Spirit descends on the apostles and the Church is born. Acts is thus the narrative of the Spirit — the promised Advocate who empowers, guides, and drives the mission toward the Gentiles, overcoming every barrier of language, culture, and law.

## Key Themes

**The Holy Spirit as the Agent of Mission.** Acts is often called the "Gospel of the Holy Spirit." The Spirit is mentioned over 70 times and is the driving force behind every major advance: the descent at Pentecost (2:1–4), the bold preaching of Peter (4:8), the selection of the seven deacons (6:3), the conversion of the Ethiopian eunuch (8:29), the inclusion of Cornelius and the Gentiles (10:44–47), the commissioning of Paul and Barnabas (13:2–4), and the direction away from Asia into Macedonia (16:6–10). The Spirit is not a passive presence but the active person of the Trinity who forms, guides, and sanctifies the Church. This makes Acts the primary biblical foundation for [[teaching/holy-spirit]] and the sacrament of Confirmation.

**The Church as the Community of Witness (ኅብረት).** Acts presents the Church not as an institution but as a living community (ኅብረት) formed by apostolic teaching, the breaking of bread (Eucharist), prayer, and the sharing of goods (2:42–47). The early believers held everything in common and cared for widows, the poor, and the sick. The Council of Jerusalem (ch. 15) models the Church's synodal character: the apostles and elders deliberate under the Spirit's guidance, and the decision to admit Gentiles without circumcision becomes binding on all the churches. Acts is thus the biblical charter for [[teaching/the-church]], demonstrating both the organic life and the hierarchical structure of the Body of Christ.

**From Israel to the Nations — The Universal Call.** Acts narrates one of the most decisive transitions in salvation history: the Gospel moves from a Jewish messianic movement within Jerusalem to a universal faith encompassing Samaritans, an Ethiopian court official, a Roman centurion (Cornelius), and ultimately the Gentile world through Paul. The conversion of Saul/Paul (ch. 9) is the pivot — the persecutor becomes the apostle to the Gentiles. Peter's vision of the unclean animals (ch. 10) and the Apostolic Council (ch. 15) confirm that salvation in Christ is not bound by the Mosaic Law. Acts closes with Paul in Rome, "proclaiming the Kingdom of God and teaching about the Lord Jesus Christ with all boldness and without hindrance" (28:31) — a deliberate open ending signaling that the mission continues in every generation.

## Key Passages

> «ነገር ግን መንፈስ ቅዱስ በእናንተ ላይ በሚወርድበት ጊዜ ኃይልን ትቀበላላችሁ፤ በኢየሩሳሌምም በይሁዳም ሁሉ በሰማርያም እስከ ምድር ዳርቻ ድረስ ምስክሮቼ ትሆናላችሁ፤» አላቸው።
> — የሐዋርያት ሥራ 1:8

Christ's parting promise and the programmatic verse for the entire book — the Spirit empowers the apostles to be witnesses from Jerusalem to the ends of the earth.

> «በሁሉም መንፈስ ቅዱስ ሞላባቸው፥ መንፈስም ይናገሩ ዘንድ እንደሰጣቸው በሌላ ልሳኖች ይናገሩ ጀመር።»
> — የሐዋርያት ሥራ 2:4

Pentecost — the descent of the Holy Spirit, the birth of the Church, and the reversal of Babel: the Spirit unites what sin had scattered.

> «በሐዋርያትም ትምህርትና በኅብረት እንጀራውንም በመቁረስ በየጸሎቱም ይተጉ ነበር።»
> — የሐዋርያት ሥራ 2:42

The four marks of the early Church: apostolic teaching (ትምህርት), fellowship/communion (ኅብረት), the breaking of bread (Eucharist), and prayer (ጸሎት) — the abiding shape of Catholic life.

> ««ጌታ ሆይ፥ ማን ነህ?» አለው። እርሱም፦ «አንተ የምታሳድደኝ እኔ ኢየሱስ ነኝ፤ የመውጊያውን ብረት ብትቃወም ለአንተ ይከብድሃል?» አለው።»
> — የሐዋርያት ሥራ 9:5

The conversion of Saul on the Damascus road — Christ identifies Himself with the Church He is being persecuted through, and the great persecutor becomes the Apostle to the Gentiles.

> «እንግዲህ ይህ የእግዚአብሔር ማዳን ለአሕዛብ እንደ ተላከ በእናንተ ዘንድ የታወቀ ይሁን፤ እነርሱ ደግሞ ይሰሙታል።»
> — የሐዋርያት ሥራ 28:28

Paul's final declaration in Rome — the salvation of God has been sent to the Gentiles, and they will listen. The book closes with the Gospel reaching the heart of the Empire.
