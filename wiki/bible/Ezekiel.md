## Overview

ትንቢተ ሕዝቅኤል (The Book of Ezekiel) is the great prophetic book of the Babylonian exile. Ezekiel ben Buzi, a priest carried into exile with King Jehoiachin in 597 BC, received his prophetic call by the river Chebar in Babylon (ch. 1). His ministry spans the darkest period of Israel's history — the final destruction of Jerusalem and the Temple in 586 BC — and yet his prophecy culminates in the most detailed vision of restoration and new life in the Old Testament. The book divides into three major sections: Oracles of Judgment against Judah and Jerusalem (chs. 1–24), Oracles against the Nations (chs. 25–32), and Oracles of Restoration and Hope (chs. 33–48). Ezekiel is both priest and prophet: his concern for the Temple, holiness, and the Glory of the LORD departing and returning to the Temple shapes the book's theology. The vision of the valley of dry bones (ch. 37), the promise of a new heart and new spirit (ch. 36), and the vision of the new Temple with the river of life flowing from it (chs. 40–48) are among the most influential prophetic texts in Scripture, directly informing the Church's theology of Baptism, the Holy Spirit, resurrection, and the heavenly Jerusalem.

## Key Themes

**The Valley of Dry Bones and Resurrection.** Ezekiel 37:1–14 is the most dramatic vision of resurrection in the Old Testament. The prophet is set down in a valley full of dry bones — a picture of Israel in exile, dead in despair: "Our bones are dried up, and our hope is lost" (37:11). At God's command, Ezekiel prophesies to the bones, and they come together, sinews and flesh cover them, and the breath (ሩአች, ruach) enters them, and they stand as a vast army. This vision is both a promise of national restoration from exile and a prophecy of bodily resurrection. The Church reads this passage in the Easter Vigil and sees in it the promise of [[teaching/forgiveness-resurrection-eternal-life]] — God raises the dead through the power of His Spirit.

**A New Heart and a New Spirit.** Ezekiel 36:24–28 is the Old Testament's most developed promise of interior transformation. God promises to sprinkle clean water, give a new heart, remove the heart of stone, and put His Spirit within His people so they walk in His statutes. This passage is the direct Old Testament background for Jesus' discourse with Nicodemus on being born of water and the Spirit (John 3:5), and for the Church's theology of [[teaching/baptism]] as the washing of regeneration and renewal in the Holy Spirit (Titus 3:5). The promise of the indwelling Spirit supports [[teaching/holy-spirit]].

**The Shepherd and the One Flock.** Ezekiel 34 delivers a devastating indictment of the shepherds of Israel who feed themselves rather than the sheep (34:1–10). In response, God declares: "I myself will be the shepherd of my sheep" (34:15), and He promises to set over them one shepherd, "my servant David" (34:23). This prophecy of the messianic shepherd — God Himself shepherding through the Davidic king — is claimed by Jesus when He declares, "I am the good shepherd" (John 10:11). The one flock under one shepherd is the foundation of [[teaching/the-church]] and [[teaching/jesus-christ-incarnation]].

**The Watchman and Personal Responsibility.** Ezekiel is appointed a watchman over the house of Israel (chs. 3, 33), responsible for warning the wicked. The theology of personal responsibility — "the soul that sins shall die" (18:4) — is developed with unprecedented clarity: each person is accountable for their own sin, not for the sins of their ancestors. Yet the heart of the watchman oracles is God's mercy: "I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but that the wicked turn from his way and live" (33:11). This call to repentance is the essence of [[teaching/moral-law-and-grace]].

## Key Passages

> «እነሆ፥ በእናንተ ላይ እስትንፋስን አገባባችኋለሁ በሕይወትም ትኖራላችሁ።»
> — ሕዝቅኤል 37:5

The valley of dry bones — God breathes the Spirit into what is dead and hopeless, and it lives. The foundational Old Testament text for the theology of resurrection and the life-giving Spirit.

> «አዲስ ልብ እሰጣችኋለሁ፤ አዲስ መንፈስ በውስጣችሁ አኖራለሁ፤ የድንጋዩንም ልብ ከሥጋችሁ አወጣለሁ፤ የሥጋ ልብ እሰጣችኋለሁ።»
> — ሕዝቅኤል 36:26

The promise of a new heart and new spirit — God's interior transformation of His people, removing the heart of stone and giving a heart of flesh.

> «ኃጢአተኛው ሰው ከመንገዱ ተመልሶ በሕይወት እንዲኖር እንጂ ኃጢአተኛው እንዲሞት አልፈቅድም፤ ይላል ጌታ እግዚአብሔር።»
> — ሕዝቅኤል 33:11

God's heart revealed — He takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked but desires repentance and life. The theological foundation of the call to conversion.

> «በላያቸውም አንድ እረኛ እሾማለሁ ... ባሪያዬ ዳዊት ነው፤ ያሰማቸዋል እርሱም እረኛ ይሆናቸዋል።»
> — ሕዝቅኤል 34:23

The one shepherd prophecy — God will shepherd His people through His servant David, the messianic king who feeds the flock.
